# Infinitesimal site: all nilpotent thickenings vs. square-zero thickenings

*A short research note, written 2026-11-07. It summarizes the standard picture and marks which claims are checked against sources and which are my own sketches.*

## Setup

Let `X` be a scheme over a base `S`; for most of this note `X` is smooth over a field `k`.

- **The full infinitesimal site `(X/S)_inf`** (Grothendieck, *Crystals and the de Rham cohomology of schemes*, 1968). Its objects are pairs `(U, T)`: `U ⊂ X` is open and `U ↪ T` is a closed `S`-immersion cut out by a **nilpotent** ideal.
- **The first-order site `(X/S)_inf^1`**. It is the same, except the ideal must have **square zero**. More generally, the order-`n` site `Inf^n` allows ideals `J` with `J^{n+1} = 0`.

So `Inf = ⋃_n Inf^n`. The question is what is lost by keeping only `n = 1`.

## 1. Lifting and deformation questions: no difference

A nilpotent thickening `T_0 ⊂ T` with `J^{N} = 0` factors as a chain of square-zero thickenings
`T_0 = V(J) ⊂ V(J^2) ⊂ ... ⊂ V(J^N) = T`.
So any lifting property can be checked one square-zero step at a time. For example, formal smoothness, unramifiedness and étaleness (EGA IV 17.1.1, stated for nilpotent ideals) are equivalent to the same conditions for square-zero ideals (Stacks Project, tag 00TI and nearby). The same reasoning is why deformation theory and derived "formal moduli" are built from square-zero extensions alone.

**Takeaway:** as a *test class for lifting problems*, square-zero extensions generate all nilpotent ones.

## 2. Crystals: a real difference

Here the two sites differ, because a crystal must be compatible with *every* thickening in the site, not just liftable along it. For smooth `X/k`, write `P^n` for the `n`-th infinitesimal neighbourhood of the diagonal in `X × X`, and `P^n(2)` for the same in `X × X × X`. A quasi-coherent crystal on `Inf^n` is the same as an `n`-stratification: an isomorphism `ε_n : p_2^*E ≅ p_1^*E` on `P^n` satisfying the cocycle condition on `P^n(2)`.

**Order 1 gives connections that need not be flat.** Write `ε_1 = 1 + θ` with `θ` in `Ω^1 ⊗ End(E)`; this is the same data as a connection `∇`. Take local coordinates `a = x_2 − x_1` and `b = x_3 − x_2` on `P^1(2)`. There all quadratic terms vanish, so

  `ε_{12} ∘ ε_{23} = (1 + θ(a))(1 + θ(b)) = 1 + θ(a) + θ(b) + θ(a)θ(b) = 1 + θ(a + b) = ε_{13}`.

The cocycle condition therefore holds automatically. *(This is my own computation; it agrees with the statement, recalled from Grothendieck and Berthelot–Ogus but not rechecked against the texts, that first-order crystals are modules with an arbitrary connection.)*

So:

  **crystals on `Inf^1`  ≅  `O_X`-modules with a connection, not necessarily integrable.**

The curvature `∇²` first appears at order 2. There, the commutator `θ(a)θ(b) − θ(b)θ(a)` no longer vanishes, and the cocycle condition forces flatness.

**The full site, in characteristic 0.** Crystals on `Inf` are modules with a full stratification. In characteristic 0, an integrable connection extends uniquely to such a stratification, by Taylor series in the operators `∂^n / n!`. So

  **crystals on `Inf`  ≅  modules with a flat connection  ≅  `D_X`-modules (`O`-quasi-coherent).**

The gap between the two sites is exactly the curvature condition. Geometrically, the first-order site sees only the tangent Lie algebroid `T_X`. A representation of that algebroid integrates to an action of the formal groupoid (the completion of `X × X` along the diagonal) exactly when it is flat.

**The full site, in characteristic `p > 0`.** Here the gap is much larger. A full stratification is an action of Grothendieck's whole ring of differential operators, including the divided operators `∂^{[p^m]}`, which are not generated by `T_X`. By Katz and Gieseker:

  **crystals on `Inf`  ≅  stratified bundles  ≅  F-divided bundles** `(E_0, E_1, ...; F^*E_{i+1} ≅ E_i)`.

Gieseker's problem asks whether these are trivial when the étale `π_1` is trivial; this is known for smooth projective varieties (Esnault–Mehta) and for proper normal varieties (Langer–Zhang 2025). In this setting:
- the first-order site gives arbitrary connections (no flatness and no `p`-curvature condition);
- integrable connections correspond instead to crystals on the **crystalline** site (PD thickenings). Those connections come with a nonzero `p`-curvature in general.

So in characteristic `p`, the three sites `Inf^1`, crystalline, and `Inf` give three genuinely different categories.

## 3. Cohomology

- **Full site, characteristic 0.** `H^*((X/k)_inf, O) ≅ H^*_dR(X/k)`. For smooth `X` this is Grothendieck's theorem; for singular `X` one uses the Hartshorne formal-completion de Rham cohomology. The Čech–Alexander computation uses the formal neighbourhoods `P^∞(n)` of the diagonals in `X^{n+1}`, and the Poincaré lemma on the formal disc is what yields the de Rham complex.
- **First-order site.** The analogous Čech–Alexander complex uses only `P^1(n) = O_{X^{n+1}}/I_Δ^2`, which is `O ⊕ Ω^{⊕n}` in degree `n`. It never reaches the higher terms needed for the formal Poincaré lemma, so I do not expect de Rham cohomology. *I have not computed it or found it in a source; treat this as an open item.*
- **Full site, characteristic `p`.** The answer is not de Rham. For example, locally `H^0` consists of the functions killed by every differential operator of positive order, which is roughly `⋂_m O_X^{p^m}`. This is why crystalline cohomology replaced infinitesimal cohomology in characteristic `p`.

## 4. Summary table

| | `Inf^1` (square-zero) | `Inf` (all nilpotent), char 0 | `Inf` (all nilpotent), char p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lifting / formal smoothness tests | same as `Inf` | — | — |
| Crystals (smooth `X`) | modules with *any* connection | flat connections / `D_X`-modules | stratified = F-divided bundles |
| Cohomology of `O` | not de Rham (expected; unchecked) | de Rham | not de Rham; crystalline is the right theory |
| Geometric shadow | tangent Lie algebroid | formal groupoid `X̂×X` ≈ `X_dR` | formal groupoid; not determined by its Lie algebroid |

**One-line answer.** Square-zero extensions generate the nilpotent ones *for lifting*, but not *for compatibility*. A crystal on the square-zero site only needs to be compatible to first order, which gives a connection. The full site imposes compatibility to all orders, which forces flatness in characteristic 0 and the whole tower of divided-power operators (F-divisibility) in characteristic `p`.
